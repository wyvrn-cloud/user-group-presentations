Catching Up
a New Device

A newly added device joins an identity mid-conversation. Where does the history it missed come from, when the mediator was never meant to keep any?

draft
Companion to "Identity DID, Device DID". That talk ends with a device enrolled; this one picks up with the next question: what does it see when it opens the app?
the gap

A mediator is a relay, not an archive.

The routing protocol has a mediator unpack a forward message and pass the payload on. Message Pickup 3.0 has the recipient acknowledge which messages "are safe to clear from the queue". Nothing in the path is meant to remember.

So a device added after the conversation started, or one that was offline for too long, can only recover history from somewhere else.

the options

Three places history could come from

Ask contacts to re-send

Every contact would have to keep and re-encrypt their side of the conversation. Leaks the fact that you added a device, and depends on them being online.

An encrypted backup service

Works when every other device is gone. But it reintroduces an archive, with its own key-management and trust questions. The registry's vaults/1.0 and swarm/1.0 (both Proposed) explore encrypted storage and durability.

Your other devices

They already hold the history and already trust the new device. The question becomes: how do they reconcile efficiently?

a constraint worth keeping

"Keep everything everywhere" doesn't fit real devices.

📱 Phone

Less storage, easier to lose. Someone may deliberately keep only the last 30 days on it.

💻 Laptop

Plenty of space. Happy to keep years of history.

Treat retention as a per-device local policy. Two devices with different policies aren't "out of sync"; one has just chosen to forget more.

one approach

Borrow rsync's technique, not just its name

Compare summaries

Per conversation: a count and the oldest/newest message ids. Cheap, and most conversations match here.

Compare chunk hashes

Only for conversations that differ: hash fixed-size chunks to find where they differ.

Diff inside the chunk

Exchange item ids or hashes for the mismatched chunk only, then transfer just the missing items.

Nothing is re-sent that both sides already hold, and each side's retention window is respected.

on the wire — a sketch

Just ordinary pairwise DIDComm messages

{
  "type": ".../sync-summary-request",
  "body": {
    "collections": [
      { "collection_id": "contacts",
        "count": 12 },
      { "collection_id": "messages:bob",
        "count": 340,
        "oldest_id": "msg-001",
        "newest_id": "msg-340" }
    ]
  }
}
  • Sent device-to-device, never broadcast.
  • The reply carries the standard thid, so request and response correlate without any new mechanism.
  • Pairwise encryption between the two devices; the mediator relays ciphertext as usual.

the other case

Scrolling back past what this device kept

Reconciliation is a background job. Scrolling is interactive: the person wants older messages now, and none of the chunking machinery helps.

  • A separate, simple request: "messages in this conversation before id X, up to N".
  • Ask several sibling devices at once and use whichever answers first.
  • Open question: keep fetched items, or show them just for this session?

what the spec already gives you

No new crypto, no new trust boundary

Confidentiality

Authcrypt between two devices of the same identity. The mediator sees only ciphertext, exactly as for any other message.

Correlation

id / thid threading ties requests to responses, and message ids give natural dedup keys.

Size limits

A device can publish max_receive_bytes, so batches can be sized to what the receiver accepts.

the trade-offs

What this approach costs

  • Hashes reveal the existence and count of messages in a range to any sibling device, even before it fetches content. Fine if every enrolled device is equally trusted; worth saying out loud.
  • It is only as good as revocation. A device that is revoked but still reachable could keep answering or asking.
  • If every device keeps a short window, old history is simply gone. One device with unbounded retention becomes the de-facto archive.
  • If the only device holding history is lost, nothing here helps. That's the backup-service trade-off again.

prior art

Messengers take different stances

Forward only

A linked device sees only messages sent after it was linked. Simplest; no history ever moves.

One-time transfer

History moves once, device to device, when migrating to a new phone. Not ongoing.

Server-side archive

Everything is stored centrally, encrypted or not. Easy sync; very different trust model.

Ongoing, peer-to-peer reconciliation with per-device retention sits somewhere between the first two.

Open questions
